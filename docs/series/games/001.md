# Video 001: games and their answer keys

The question: "is the player in the air?". Each game below stores the
answer in its own memory, which is the answer key; the neuron reads a
different number (the player's height), and the two-number neuron also
reads his vertical speed. Every address is cited to its published RAM map and
verified in Greenstone by a test in `src/nes/gym/series/ram.rs`, on the
exact ROM file named by its SHA-1.

## Super Mario Bros. (1985)

ROM: `roms2/super_mario_bros.nes`, SHA-1
`ea343f4e445a9050d4b4fbac2c77d0693b1d0922` (mapper 0). Source: Data
Crystal, "Super Mario Bros./RAM map",
https://datacrystal.tcrf.net/wiki/Super_Mario_Bros./RAM_map (revision
70369), which states it applies to the "(JU) (PRG0)" dump.

| Address | Meaning (per the map) | Verified |
|---|---|---|
| `$001D` | Player "float" state: 0 standing, 1 airborne by jumping, 2 airborne by walking off a ledge, 3 sliding down the flagpole | test |
| `$00CE` | Player y position on screen (measured from a point above his head: in the sprite table small Mario's feet are at `$00CE` + 32, so 176 on the floor puts his feet at screen row about 208; the byte runs past 240 in a pit). Animations should draw depth d at screen row d + 32 (tested: standing on the floor, small Mario's lower sprites sit at depth + 24 in the sprite table's copy at `$0200`, from the offset at `$06E4`) | test |
| `$009F` | Player vertical velocity in whole pixels, signed (`$FB` a normal jump, `$05` fastest fall) | test |
| `$0086`, `$006D` | Player x on screen; horizontal position in the level | (used by Greenstone's reward code) |
| `$07DD`–`$07E2` | Mario's score, BCD, one digit per byte: millions down to tens (the ones digit is always 0) | test |
| `$0009` | Frame counter (wraps) | for 003 |
| `$0016`–`$001A` | Enemy type, five slots (a category ID: `$06` Goomba, `$00` green Koopa, …) | for 003 |

Airborne means `$001D` is 1 or 2.

| `$00A2` | Player vertical position in levels | test |

Booting into 1-1: Start, Start (one player), then on the world map hold

## Castlevania (1986/1987)

ROM: `roms2/castlevania.nes`, SHA-1
`7a20c44f302fb2f1b7adffa6b619e3e1cae7b546` (mapper 2). Source: Data
Crystal, "Castlevania (NES, Famicom Disk System)/RAM map",
https://datacrystal.tcrf.net/wiki/Castlevania_(NES,_Famicom_Disk_System)/RAM_map
(revision 71689).

| Address | Meaning (per the map) | Verified |
|---|---|---|
| `$046C` | Simon's state: 00 walking, 01 jumping (and air attack), 02 ground attack, 03 ducking, 04 climbing stairs, 05 knocked back, 06 approaching stairs, 07 dropping, 08 dead, 09 stunned | test |
| `$003F` | Simon's real y coordinate | test |
| `$03C4` | Vertical speed (per-object array; stays 0 during Simon's jump in Greenstone, so the jump isn't stored as a speed) | observed |
| `$038C` | X coordinate (per-object array; Simon is slot 0) | test |
| `$07FC`–`$07FE` | Score, three bytes, encoding not stated | not used in 001 |
| `$015B` | Subweapon: 08 dagger, 09 cross, 0B holy water, 0D axe, 0F stopwatch (a category ID) | for 003 |

Airborne means `$046C` is 01 (jumping), 05 (knocked back: Simon flies
backwards through the air) or 07 (dropping; never seen in our
recordings). Castlevania's
jump has no speed byte: its height follows a fixed arc, so the same
question has a differently shaped answer than in the Mario games, a
multi-game point for the script.

Booting: Start at the title, Start again; play begins about frame 770.
Pressing Start during play pauses it.

## Other well-known candidates (not in `roms2/`)

Documented on Data Crystal with an airborne signal, for Nate to supply
if a video wants them: **Ninja Gaiden** (`$0084` bit 0x04 "jumping or
falling"; y `$008A`; vertical speed `$0089`/`$0087`), **Contra** (`$00A0`

(`$0032` the tile under his feet: 00 air, 01 ground, …; y `$04A0`),
**Metroid** (vertical speed `$0308`). Super Mario Bros. 2's map documents
no airborne byte.

## Comparing with zero, in the game's own code

For `why-zero`: Super Mario Bros. counts a timer down and branches on the
result with no compare, where a test against any other number needs one.
From doppelganger's disassembly (SMBDIS.ASM), checked against the bytes of
`roms2/super_mario_bros.nes` (PRG at file offset 16, mapped at `$8000`):

| Address | Bytes | Instructions | |
|---|---|---|---|
| `$80F6` | `AD 47 07 F0 05` | `lda TimerControl` / `beq DecTimers` | load the timer; branch if it's zero |
| `$80FB` | `CE 47 07 D0 19` | `dec TimerControl` / `bne NoDecTimers` | count it down; branch on the result, no compare |
| `$8022` | `AD FF 07` | `lda WarmBootValidation` | load a value |
| `$8025` | `C9 A5 D0 02` | `cmp #$a5` / `bne ColdBoot` | to test it against $A5 (165), a compare comes first: it subtracts $A5 and sets the zero flag if nothing is left |

(`TimerControl` is `$0747`.) `DEC` sets the zero and negative flags from
its result, and `BNE`/`BEQ` branch on the zero flag; `CMP` subtracts its
operand from the accumulator without keeping the result, setting the
same flags (mass:werk's 6502 instruction set). So testing against any
other number is subtracting it and testing against zero: the script's
`why-zero-3`.

## Recordings

Made with `examples/series_record.rs` into
`target/series/001-one-neuron/data/<game>/<seed>/`: 18,000 frames (five
minutes) each, a random player (button combinations held for 4 + a
geometric number of frames, mean about 20), restarting from the first
frame of play after every death; `meta.json` lists the restart frames.
The random player's sessions never leave the opening of the first level. The rule teacher's recording clears 1-1 (348 frames sliding down the flagpole, `$001D` = 3; those frames are the game moving Mario, so the lab leaves them out) and spends 4,060 of its 18,000 frames in 1-2, where the floor is also at depth 176. The footage take per game is
the first 3,600 frames of seed 1 (recordings are deterministic, so it
matches that seed's data frame for frame).

Corrections after the council's first round: Super Mario Bros. 3's death
is now detected when it begins (`$058C`, found by experiment: it turns on
the frame a death starts and stays on through the death animation),
where it had been detected only when the lives counter dropped, about
200 frames later, so 37–46% of each recording had been a dying Mario
labelled "on the ground". Castlevania's answer key now counts knockback
(state 05) as in the air: Simon flies backwards through the air when hit.

| Game | Seed | In the air | Restarts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Super Mario Bros. | 1 / 2 / 3 | 43.3% / 47.3% / 45.4% | 35 / 41 / 34 |
| Super Mario Bros. (rule teacher 40,3,3,9) | – | 52.7% | 7 |

## Results (`series.lab.exp001`: chosen on seeds 1–2, tested on seed 3)

Is the player in the air?

| Game | Always "on the ground" | One cut on height | Best line, height and speed | Rule "speed isn't zero" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Super Mario Bros. | 54% | 94% (cut 176, the floor) | 94% | 97% |
| Super Mario Bros. 3 | 50% | 87% (cut 128, its floor) | 87% | 99% |
| Castlevania | 67% | 92% (cut 176) | – (no speed byte) | – |

- Not a fluke of the sessions it was chosen on: choosing the cut on any
  one session alone gives the same cut in every game (176, 128, 176), and
  Super Mario Bros.' cut scores 93.9% / 94.2% / 94.1% on sessions 1 / 2 / 3.
  The rule teacher's recording (steadier play) also picks 176, and scores
  96.7% with it.
- Most of the one-cut neuron's mistakes are Mario standing on something
  higher than the floor (a pipe, a block, a platform): 92% in Super Mario
  Bros. (962 of 1,046 on session 3), 88% in Super Mario Bros. 3.
- Super Mario Bros. counts only frames of play (`in_play`: player state
  `$000E` is 08). The rest are the game moving Mario: dying (which sets
  the in-the-air byte while he stands on the floor), the freezes while
  he grows (09) or shrinks after being hit (0A, whose first frames have
  the same fault), the flagpole, intermissions and pipes. Masked: 31, 39
  and 144 frames of sessions 1–3, and 4,746 (26%) of the rule teacher's.
  In the script this moved the share above from 88% to 92%, always
  saying "on the ground" from 55% to 54%, and in the landing section the
  raised share from 60% to 61% and the bounces-or-pits share from 91% to
  94%; the numbers in this doc are after masking.
- A second number (vertical speed) doesn't improve a straight line for
  this question: speed matters only when it *isn't zero*, which takes two
  cuts. That's 004's example (moved there from 001).
- Castlevania: the slot its RAM map calls vertical speed (`$03C4`) was 0
  on every frame of every recording. Its "jump state" (`$0488`) counts up
  to 39 through each jump: the game keeps where Simon is in the jump, not
  a speed.

Two numbers that both matter (Super Mario Bros.): Mario is in the air;
will he land within 10 frames?

| Always "no" | One cut on height | One cut on speed | Best line, both |
|---|---|---|---|
| 75% | 75% | 84% | 91% |

The best line tilts: lower, and falling faster, both point to "lands
soon".

The landing border in whole numbers: height + 5 × speed > 152, also 91%.
For the whole-number border, 85% of the moments it misses before a
landing are before landings onto something raised (for the unrounded
border, 90%; of all its mistakes, 61%). For the whole-number border too, raised landings are 61% of all its mistakes. Its false alarms ("lands soon" when he doesn't land within 10 frames; 216 on session 3) are 94% bounces or pits: 125 times he bounces back up before landing (off a stomped enemy: reviewers matched all 17 bounce events to stomps; about 10 of the frames come while he's still rising from a stomp already made), 78 times he falls below the screen into a pit, and 13 times he lands later (`false_alarm_kind` in `exp001.py`; the freeze while a hurt Mario shrinks doesn't end its look-ahead).

For 002, not 001: Rosenblatt's learning rule, run on the in-the-air
question's two inputs, never settles, because the data can't be split
cleanly, and where it stops is chaotic: masking a few dozen frames, or
only changing the order floating-point sums are taken in, moved its test
score in Super Mario Bros. from 84% to 30%, against the 94% of the
setting chosen by search. That instability is 002's material.

Mario's top score in these recordings: 1,300.
