# Video 002: Every mistake moves the line

The question is 001's landing question, unchanged: Mario is in the air;
will he land within the next 10 frames? The neuron reads his depth
(`$00CE`) and vertical speed (`$009F`); the answer key is his float state
(`$001D`). Addresses, ROM and verification are in `001.md`. What's new is
who chooses the border: in 001 we searched every leaning line and kept the
best; here the neuron moves its own line, one moment at a time.

## The rules

Nate's `ndouglas/linear-regression` (built from Serrano, *Grokking Machine
Learning*) has three tricks for fitting a line *through* points. 002 needs
a line *between* two colours of dot, so each trick is translated. Labels
are −1 (doesn't land soon) and +1 (lands soon); the line says +1 when
w·x + b > 0.

| In the repo (fitting) | For a border | The nudge, after looking at one moment |
|---|---|---|
| Simple trick | Simple trick | Only if wrong: move each weight a fixed step by the *sign* of its input |
| Absolute trick | Perceptron rule (Rosenblatt, 1958) | Only if wrong: move the weights by the input, times a fixed step |
| Square trick | Widrow–Hoff / LMS (1960) | Always: move the weights by the input, times the error (label − w·x − b) |

To check before any of it is said: the dates and attributions, and that
Serrano's perceptron and logistic tricks match these forms.

## The experiment (`tools/series/lab/exp002.py`)

Numbers in `tools/series/videos/002-every-mistake/numbers.json`; the
rules are in `tools/series/lab/rules.py`, with tests in
`tools/series/tests/test_rules.py`. Learned on sessions 1–2 (16,135
moments in the air), scored on session 3 (8,072; lands soon 25%). Inputs
standardized on the moments learned from; weights start at zero; 30
passes, each visiting every moment once in a shuffled order; 10 orders
per rule.

| Rule | Score on session 3 | Settles? (median over the last 10 passes) |
|---|---|---|
| Search (001) | 91% | (no learning) |
| Simple trick, per moment, step 0.01 | 75–89% across orders | No: swings 25 points, turns 117° |
| Perceptron, per moment, step 0.01 | 70–90% | No: swings 17 points, turns 127° |
| Square trick, per moment, step 0.001 | 87–88% | Nearly: 1 point, 3° |
| Square trick, whole batch, step 0.1 | 87% | Yes; at the least-squares line |
| Logistic trick, whole batch | 88% | Yes |

- One perceptron run's score after each of passes 21–30: 79, 86, 75, 73,
  86, 83, 85, 88, 87, 90%.
- Leaving out 30 random moments (of 16,135), same order: the
  perceptron ends anywhere from 69% to 86% over 10 such drops.
- The settled borders lean about 35° from search's (standardized units)
  and answer differently from it on 6% (square) and 4% (logistic) of
  session 3's moments.
- The square trick's 87% doesn't depend on the step chosen: steps
  0.0003 and 0.003 give 87–88% too.

More measured (same run):

- **Batching doesn't stop the wobble.** The batch perceptron (every
  mistake's nudge averaged into one step) scores 43–90% over its last
  100 steps.
- **Remembering does.** Keeping the run's best line (pocket, judged on
  the moments learned from): 89–90%. Averaging every line it passed
  through: 89%.
- **Which moments keep it moving:** the busiest tenth of moments make
  45% of all nudges; 30% of moments never cause one.
- **Right by too much:** 16% of moments learned from are answered right
  by the least-squares line but overshoot its target, and it still pulls
  toward them; 97% of them are rising (median speed −3, against −1
  overall; median depth 123, against 133). The candidate cause of the
  lean.
- **The two maps** (every border as angle × offset; saved to
  `target/series/002-every-mistake/maps.npz`): the mistakes map's lowest
  point scores 91% (search's), the squared-error map's 87%, and they're
  34.5° apart. The bowl's lowest point is checked to be the
  least-squares border. Rendered, the squared-error map is smooth; the
  mistakes map has hard-edged terraces and a narrow best region with a
  cliff along one side. The terraces are partly because depth and speed
  are whole numbers: many moments share a dot, so crossing it moves the
  count in a visible step.

Checked before the numbers are written (the run refuses otherwise):

- The perceptron's border doesn't change with its step size (from a
  zero start a step only scales the weights; 001's "scaling the weights
  doesn't move the border").
- The batch square trick comes to rest exactly at the least-squares
  solution, solved directly.
- Coding the answers −1/+1 or 0/1 gives the same square-trick border
  (with the border halfway between the codes).
- The squared-error map's lowest point is the least-squares border
  (within a degree).

## The episode's spine (Nate, 2026-11-01)

- Be honest about the wobble: the perceptron aims at the right thing
  (mistakes) but can't settle, because with "mostly on one side" some
  moment is always wrong.
- The square trick settles, but aims at something slightly different
  (squared error, which also listens to moments it already gets right),
  and stops short of search's line.
- Why the settling kind won (corrected 2026-11-01, ideonomy pass 2):
  not batching. The perceptron can be batched too, and the batch
  perceptron still wobbles (57–90% over its last 100 steps; scratch).
  What the square trick has is a *slope*: its nudges shrink as it nears
  its target. Every border's mistake count, mapped, is a staircase with
  no slope; its squared error is a smooth bowl. A slope is also what
  learning through many layers needs (later: gradients), and aiming at
  mistakes directly is intractable with many inputs (NP-hard in general;
  to check), which is why everyone aims at a bowl. (Nate had assumed the
  repo's last trick was the GPU-friendly one; Claude then said batching
  was the difference; both are corrected here.)
